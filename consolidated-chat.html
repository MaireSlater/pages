Exciting title

With all of the attention being paid to the way AI interacts with CRA pages, you may be pro-actively working on your content, or you may have been asked to ensure your pages are optimized.

The idea of optimizing for Chatbots is fairly new. What experts have discovered, though, is that we already know how to do it. The guidance we already have in place is exactly the guidance we need for AI optimization.

AI likes user-centered, accessible content

The surprising tldr: Chatbots prefer content that is user-centered and accessible.

Studies have found that websites that meet accessibility standards perform about 25% better in AI search tools than websites that are not accessible.

This means that if you are following the Canada.ca Content Style Guide, your page is already positioned for success.

Follow the Canada.ca Content Style Guide

The Canada.ca Content Style Guide acts as an instruction manual for creating web pages that can be easily found, understood, and used. As well as creating consistency across Canada.ca, this ensures pages are optimized and accessible for all our readers, including, it turns out, AI chatbots

The following are highlights from our guidance that have been found to increase page performance in AI search tools.

Communicate clearly with plain language

Plain language is a writing approach that uses clear wording, structure, and design to make information more accessible and easy to read. It doesn’t oversimplify or strip meaning from content. Instead, plain language content is clear, concise, and designed to save the intended audience time and effort.

What you can do: Applying plain language principles

  • Be direct and brief. Short sentences (under 20 words) and paragraphs (under 4 sentences) are easier for AI models to extract as definitive answers.
  • Use familiar words. Learn what words people are using to search for your content and use those words on your pages. Avoid using jargon or words that are understood internally to have a CRA-specific meaning that is different from the general definition. If third parties are publishing web content that is more easily understood than our pages, LLMs may prioritize those pages over our own..
    • Conversational language outperforms jargon. AI systems process direct, plain language better than corporate speak. Write like you’re explaining something to an intelligent colleague over coffee, not presenting at a board meeting.
      • Forbes
  • Address the bilingual gap. French accuracy in popular AI tools has scored less than half as well as English has. Have your French content properly translated and QA’d by a subject matter expert to make sure program-specific words and ideas have been translated consistently.

Structure your content to be scannable

Reading behaviour is different on a web page than on a printed page. Rather than reading every word, people scan web pages for information that will help them complete the task they came to do.

What you can do: Designing for scannability

  • Lead with summaries. Place a concise summary or introduction at the top of the page, under the H1. Starting with a page introduction improves LLM comprehension and helps it decide if the page is relevant.
  • Write useful page titles and headings. Page titles and headings should be unique and written in plain language. They should not be formed as questions, but should instead provide answers or describe the section that follows.  Use a proper hierarchy of HTML headings (h1, h2, h3, etc.) to structure content logically and guide the AI through the information flow.
  • Prioritize tasks. Focus each page on a single, task-based topic. Add CRA-specific value such as definitions, examples, decision guidance, eligibility details, and next steps instead of repeating generic background information.
  • Remove trivial information. Unnecessary content, like welcome messages or background information, can make relevant information harder to find. Readers (human or AI) may struggle to distinguish between key points and filler. Keeping trivial information forces the AI to process more text which could lead to a less a concise and less helpful AI response.
    • AI search changes the scale, not the nature, of the problem. A page that once confused a small number of visitors can now shape an answer seen by thousands.
      • Jumping Elephants

Develop a content management strategy

Chatbots don’t merely quote page content, they synthesize information from multiple sources. The way we manage our content needs to signal to AI systems that our pages are trustworthy enough to use as a primary source.

What you can do: Managing your content

  • Avoid redundant, outdated, or trivial (ROT) content. If ROT is found and used by chatbots, it could lower the quality of responses and feed inaccurate information to users. This can easily cause confusion or harm the CRA’s reputation.
  • Do a content inventory. AI tools combine information from multiple sources and don’t recognize departmental, branch, or team boundaries. So, avoid working in silos. Present consistent, accurate, and authoritative information by taking inventory of all content related to your topic across the Government of Canada website. Coordinate with other teams to create a unified presence on your topic.
  • Rethink success metrics. There has already been a huge drop of traffic to Canada.ca pages because of users bypassing websites when AI provides answers. KPIs now need to focus on the effectiveness of information delivery, not just clicks.

Optimize technical performance

AI systems can read the technical elements of a web page in a way that a human reader can’t. How a page handles the technological allowances and constraints influences how likely a chatbot is to interact with it.

What you can do: Taking advantage of technical functionality

  • Check for mobile compatibility. Around 50% of (human) users come to our pages on their mobile devices. Make sure your page is accessible and readable on a smaller screen as AI tools prioritize well-performing websites.
  • Provide alternative formats for interactive elements.

    Some AI tools can’t access field flows, wizards, or calculators. Create alternate HTML versions of interactive content and tools that AI systems can read and share.
